Raising Awareness for Girls' Education

By Nhyira Frempong · March 20, 2026 · 4 min read
← Back to Blog

Education is a fundamental human right. Yet across the world — particularly in rural Africa and the Middle East — millions of girls are being denied this right every single day. Not because they don't want to learn. Not because they lack the ability. But because of poverty, cultural barriers, conflict, and systemic inequality that has been ignored for far too long.

At GNET, we believe that awareness is the first step toward change. Before we can fix a problem, the world needs to know it exists. And the problem is urgent.

The Reality Girls Face

131 million girls are out of school worldwide. In sub-Saharan Africa, only 36% of girls complete secondary school. In parts of the Middle East, girls are actively prevented from attending school by law, tradition, or fear. Many girls who do enroll drop out before finishing — forced into early marriage, domestic labor, or simply unable to afford school fees, uniforms, or supplies.

These are not just statistics. These are real girls with dreams, with potential, with the capacity to become doctors, engineers, teachers, and leaders — if only they were given the chance.

How This Connects to the UN Sustainable Development Goals

Girls' education is not just a gender issue — it is a global development issue. The United Nations has outlined 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) to build a better world by 2030. GNET's work directly supports several of these goals:

SDG 1

No Poverty

Educated girls earn more, lifting families and communities out of poverty for generations.

SDG 3

Good Health

Educated girls make better health decisions for themselves, their children, and their communities.

SDG 4

Quality Education

Ensuring inclusive and equitable quality education for every girl, everywhere.

SDG 5

Gender Equality

Education is the most powerful tool for achieving real gender equality worldwide.

SDG 10

Reduced Inequalities

Closing the education gap between boys and girls reduces inequality at every level of society.

SDG 17

Partnerships

GNET collaborates with 45 NGOs globally to scale our reach and deepen our impact.

When we educate a girl, we are not just changing one life. Research shows that every extra year of schooling increases a girl's earning potential by up to 25%. An educated mother is more likely to send her own children to school. An educated woman is more likely to participate in civic life, push back against injustice, and contribute to her nation's economy.

Educating girls is quite simply one of the highest-return investments a society can make.

What Awareness Actually Looks Like

Raising awareness isn't just posting on social media — though that matters too. Real awareness means changing how communities, governments, and institutions think about girls' education. It means running seminars in rural villages where families learn why keeping their daughter in school is better for everyone. It means working with local leaders to shift cultural norms. It means showing up, consistently, and refusing to let this issue be forgotten.

GNET has conducted 120+ educational seminars across 12 countries. We show up in the communities that are hardest to reach, because those are the communities that need us most.
